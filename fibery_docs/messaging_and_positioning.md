## The Category: Arrival Ops

Owns the critical period between **offer accepted → Day One**. NYCbound is the first company to operationalize it.

**Category hook:** "You have RevOps. You have DevOps. Why don't you have Arrival Ops?"

Only lands if immediately followed by: what happens without it, and what changes with it.

**What it's NOT:**

* Not Global Mobility (handles compliance, not the landing)
* Not RMCs (manage the move, not readiness)
* Not DSPs (orientation, not readiness)
* Not just brokers (units, not outcomes)

### Why This Category Exists Now

Arrival Ops didn't exist as a category before because the workforce didn't require it. Five forces created the gap:

* **Distributed teams** mean more relocations than ever
* **RTO mandates** are pushing inbound moves to NYC
* **Global hiring** has made arrival logistics dramatically more complex
* **NYC is the hardest landing city in the U.S.** — unique rules, velocity, and friction that don't exist anywhere else
* **Companies can't afford 3 weeks of lost productivity** at modern comp levels

Legacy categories (Global Mobility, RMCs, DSPs, brokers) each manage *fragments* of relocation. None of them protect productivity or readiness. Arrival Ops fills that gap.

## Core Problem & Solution

**The problem:** Relocating talent to NYC quietly destroys onboarding. Companies lose \~3 weeks of productivity — \~$12K wasted for every $200K hire. And when a relocation fails (40% of the time), the cost balloons to **$701K in missed Year 1 value** on a $165K SWE.

**Per-hire productivity drag (every relocation):**

* $12K direct productivity loss (3 weeks at \~$800/day)
* $5K manager time drain
* $3K team coordination cost
* **Total: $10K–$20K per relocation**

**Failed-relo ROI loss (40% of relocations):**

* For a $165K SWE: **$701,404 in missed Year 1 value** vs. a hire that works out
* Source: [Ramp Calculator](https://pinpointe.nyc/nyc-hiring-ramp-calculator/)

* Domestic: $10K stipend → actually costs $22K
* International: $10K stipend → actually costs $30K+

**NYCbound's solution:** One point of contact. Everything owned between offer accepted and Day One. We don't promise a specific turnaround time — what we guarantee is that the 1–3 months of extra ramp delay a DIY move creates gets recovered.

## How Arrival Ops Differs From Legacy Models

> DSPs support *orientation.* Arrival Ops supports *readiness.*

### Not Just Brokers

**What most brokers do:**

* Find apartments
* Have limited availability
* Cannot support international moves
* Cannot handle complex cases (families, visas, executives)
* Are transaction-driven

> Brokers secure *units.* Arrival Ops secures *outcomes.*

* **Benefit:** Deep knowledge of neighborhoods, landlord requirements, application timing, guarantor options, no-credit workarounds.
* **Why it matters:** Out-of-state hires don't know that "luxury FiDi building" means a 45-minute commute, or that they need 80× rent guarantor without US credit. National vendors don't either.
* **Messaging:** "Your hire from Austin doesn't know the NYC rental game. We do, and we handle it before they waste 3 weeks learning the hard way."

### 5. Cost Avoidance: Productivity Loss

* **Benefit:** Eliminates \~$12K in lost productivity per $200K hire (3 weeks of unproductive ramp).
* **Why it matters:** Multiply across a Q1 hiring plan = six figures in wasted onboarding time.
* **Messaging:** "Three weeks of apartment hunting equals $12K in lost productivity per hire. We eliminate that."

### 6. Cost Avoidance: Failed-Relo ROI

* **Benefit:** Reduces relocation failure risk. Each failed SWE relo = **$701K in missed Year 1 value** per the [Ramp Calculator](https://pinpointe.nyc/nyc-hiring-ramp-calculator/). 40% of relocations fail (Jobera).

* **Messaging:** "When a relocation fails, you're looking at $701K in missed Year 1 value. The math vs. structured support isn't close."

### 7. The Onboarding Killer Problem

* **Benefit:** New hires can focus 100% on ramping instead of splitting attention between learning the job and finding housing.
* **Why it matters:** Split focus = poor onboarding experience + higher 90-day churn risk.
* **Messaging:** "Your new PM is supposed to be ramping on Q1 OKRs, not taking 2-hour lunch breaks to tour apartments in Bushwick."

## Key Objections & Responses

**"We already use a global mobility firm"**<br>→ They outsource the apartment search to local brokers like us and charge 3× for the same outcome. Worth comparing?

**"We already have an RMC"**<br>→ Two ways we fit. We can plug in as the NYC execution layer, sitting under your RMC for New York moves only. Or, depending on how much you actually lean on the RMC, we can replace it for NYC and handle housing, settling-in, and move coordination directly. The one thing we don't do is expense management or inventory management — if those are core to how you use the RMC, we slot in alongside it rather than replace it.

**"Our employees can find their own apartments"**<br>→ They can — but it takes 3–4 weeks and costs $12K per $200K hire in lost productivity. And when 40% of those relocations fail, you're looking at $701K in missed Year 1 value per failed hire.

**"We only have 1–2 relocations per year"**<br>→ Low volume doesn't lower the per-hire risk. A single failed NYC relo is $701K in missed Year 1 value — you don't need volume to justify getting one right. And you engage us per move, no retainer or annual minimum, so there's nothing to carry between relocations.

**"Isn't that expensive for an apartment search?"**<br>→ Home First is just the standard NYC broker fee — every apartment here comes with one, usually 12–15% of annual rent. The difference is we handle everything: neighborhood selection, tours, paperwork, guarantor setup, lease negotiation. Without us, your employee spends 3–4 weeks doing it themselves, roughly $11.5K in lost productivity. The higher tiers (Complete and Executive Arrival) add the settling-in and white-glove layer on top.

**"We're not hiring in NYC right now"**<br>→ Expanding in 6–12 months? Happy to be a resource when the time comes.

## Sales Messaging Principles

1. **Lead with the problem they don't know they have.** HR buyers think their RMC is "fine." Make the invisible pain visible first.
2. **Differentiated storytelling formula:** Current solution → predictable failure → NYCbound's approach → better outcome.
3. **Pass the "So What" test.** Connect every differentiator to a human consequence.
4. **Rational + Emotional — both required.** Rational: cost, time, failure rate. Emotional: the HR person still managing the housing crisis, the spouse who hates New York.
5. **Before-and-after per persona.** The before = hook. The after = close.
6. **Cold email standard:** Can they read it and think "that happened last week"?
7. **Enterprise buyers are risk-averse careerists.** Make *not switching* feel riskier than switching.
8. **Signal-based outreach.** First GM hire posting = highest-conviction signal — but use it as a *stage* signal, not a 24-hour trigger. Add to the watchlist when it posts; reach out once the person is in seat (\~30-60 days). See [ICP and Qualification](https://pinpointe.fibery.io/Sales_Intelligence/476).
9. **Not every audience is a buyer.** Some are distribution channels (e.g., VC Platform Talent — see [Buyer Personas](https://pinpointe.fibery.io/Sales_Intelligence/477)). Adjust messaging accordingly: distributors get assets to share, not pitches to act on.

## Messaging by Audience and Channel

Different audiences need different messaging. Different channels need different formats.

### By Audience

**HR / People Ops:** Pain-and-relief framing. "We offload the NYC chaos so you focus on hiring outcomes." Use Arrival Ops language. Reference [People Ops play](https://pinpointe.fibery.io/Sales_Intelligence/480).

**CFO / Finance:** Confident, data-driven, never point-blank or accusatory. Leads with a quantified gap and a stated fix, not a verdict — see the rebuilt [CFO / Finance Leader play](https://pinpointe.fibery.io/Sales_Intelligence/480) for the exact framing. Use Arrival Ops language.

**CEO / Founder:** Plain language only. "De-risk landings. Protect early productivity. Your hire codes from Day One." Peer-to-peer founder voice. NO Arrival Ops.

**Recruiters / Talent Partners:** Pipeline protection. "Even when relocation policy sits elsewhere, the ghosted offer lands on your desk." Use Arrival Ops language.

**Global Mobility Leaders:** Complementary, never competitive. Positioning: "We're your NYC execution module. We plug into your existing stack and take NYC off the exceptions list." Pain-and-relief only. Use Arrival Ops language.

**VC Platform Talent (distribution channel, not buyer):** Asset-driven. "Here's a tool you can ship to your portcos." Not a sales pitch.

**Real estate / brokerage audience:** No Arrival Ops. Talk about Pinpointe as the boutique NYC firm that built a B2B program.

### By Channel

**Verbal / quick** (introductions, networking, hallway conversations):

* "We make talent ready on Day One — not week three."
* "We handle the arrival moment so your team doesn't lose weeks."
* Keep it under 10 seconds.

**Cold email:**

* See [Email Plays](https://pinpointe.fibery.io/Sales_Intelligence/480) for full sequence frameworks per persona.
* Universal rule: preview text earns the open. Lead with a research insight or a number, never with industry facts they already know.

**LinkedIn posts:**

* Personal, founder-led voice. Hooks lead with a story or a number.

* Always tie back to a specific business outcome (productivity, ROI, retention).

**Social (X, general social):**

* "Talent relocation is chaotic because no one owns the arrival moment. NYCbound changes that."
* Shorter than LinkedIn. Punchier.

**Sales conversations / discovery:**

* Lead with discovery questions, not pitch.
* "What did your last NYC relocation that didn't work out actually cost you?" (Ramp Calculator framing)
* Use [Case Studies](https://pinpointe.fibery.io/Sales_Intelligence/487) to anchor scenarios.

**Pitch decks / sales presentations:**

* Before-and-after per persona pain.
* Lead with the $701K Year 1 ROI gap on failed relos.
* Anchor with 1–2 case studies from [Case Studies](https://pinpointe.fibery.io/Sales_Intelligence/487).
* End with the comparison: \~$15K–$46K NYCbound (depending on tier and salary band) vs. $701K failed-relo ROI loss. "The math is not close."

**Website hero / landing page:**

* Big claim + supporting proof.
* Example: "We make talent arrival work." (tagline) → 30 days to 5 days, $701K protected. *(FLAG — any "X% cheaper than RMCs" proof point is under review pending pricing research.)*

**Event / roundtable spoken pitch:**

* Storytelling lead. Use sharper emotional anchors ("trauma with a start date").
* Que PASA framework (Problem, Anchor, Solution, Ask).
* See the 60-second pitch structure used at Women.NYC Deal Day.

**Partner pitch** (different from buyer pitch):

* Frame around mutual customer benefit, not just NYCbound.
* "Your customers are dealing with NYC relo. We can take that off your plate."
* Asset-first: offer the calculator and one-pager up front.

## Pricing & Tiers

NYCbound pricing is tiered by service depth and scaled by the relocating employee's salary band. Three tiers, three salary bands. The corporate rate is a 15% broker fee. An individual rate (12% broker fee) exists only for case-study / social cross-promotion deals and is not used in standard B2B pricing.

**Corporate list prices include 10% negotiation room. Floors shown — do not discount below the floor.**

### The Three Tiers

* **Home First** — Neighborhood advisory, apartment search, tours, application management, lease execution. Broker fee only, no service layer. Internal use only — never lead with this tier in marketing; only sell it when it's the only way in, or as an upsell path toward Complete/Executive Arrival.
* **Complete Arrival** — Permanent housing broker fee + temporary housing broker fee (½ price in the bundle) + SSN setup, bank account, driver's license advisory, utility setup, and arrival coordination.
* **Executive Arrival** — Everything in Complete Arrival, plus immigration partner coordination, moving company coordination, furnishings / interior design referrals, and family & school advisory.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Home First** | $9,000 | $15,000 (floor $13,500) | $25,000 (floor $22,500) |
| **Complete Arrival** | $15,000 (floor $14,000) | $20,000 (floor $18,000) | $35,000 (floor $32,000) |
