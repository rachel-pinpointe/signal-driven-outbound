## The Headline Numbers (Lead with These)

The most shocking, most useful numbers from the [Ramp Calculator](https://pinpointe.nyc/nyc-hiring-ramp-calculator/). Use in pitches, content, and Email 2–3.

**Software Engineer ($165K base, $216,459 all-in Yr1 cost):**

| Scenario | Year 1 Net Value | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local hire, works out (benchmark) | **$952,547** | Best case, 9-month ramp |
| DIY relocation, works out | **$835,647** | 12-month ramp, one month slower |
| Local hire, doesn't work out | **$660,296** | Terminated M4, replacement ramping M7–12 |
| **Relocation, doesn't work out** | **$251,144** | **40% of relocations. Hire terminated M4, hiring restarts from zero** |

### THE KEY NUMBER FOR OUTREACH

A failed relocation costs you **$701,404** compared to a hire that works out ($952,547 minus $251,144).

**This is the number to use.** Not $953K (that's the ceiling of what's possible). Not $116K (that's the Ramp Gap — methodology). The actual cost of a failed relo is **$701K in missed Year 1 value.**

*Documented exception: the rebuilt CFO / Finance Leader play (Sept 2026) deliberately leads with the \~$117K gap instead of $701K — it's a considered persona-specific choice (the guaranteed gap is the counterintuitive hook), not an error. $701K still appears in that play, just not always first. This general rule still holds for every other persona and context.*

**The framing that works in outreach:**

> "40% of relocations fail. When that happens, you're looking at $701K in missed Year 1 value compared to a hire that would have worked out. Most companies don't track this number."

## Ramp Calculator Methodology

Built with YOC (Your Opportunity Co). Live at: https://pinpointe.nyc/nyc-hiring-ramp-calculator/<br>Blog post: https://pinpointe.nyc/the-ramp-gap-how-to-calculate-what-nyc-relocation-costs-you-in-year-1-value/

Each scenario shows Year 1 Net Value — what the hire returns to the organization by end of Year 1, after all costs. Benchmark assumes 9 months to full productivity (Deel).

### The Four Scenarios

| Scenario | What it models |
| --- | --- |
| **Local hire, works out** | Standard onboarding, no relocation. 9-month ramp, reaches full productivity by month 9. Year 1 Net Value = full potential. The default benchmark. |
| **DIY relocation, works out** | Relocating hire handling the move themselves — DIY, lump-sum stipend, no structured support. 12-month ramp, one month slower than the local-hire benchmark, because they're splitting focus between the job and the move. Loss of \~$117K vs. local best case. This is the default outcome today, absent NYCbound — closing this exact gap is the whole value proposition. |
| **Local hire, doesn't work out** | Skipping relocation by hiring locally — when that hire doesn't work out. Terminated at month 4, replacement hired months 7–12 and still ramping. |
| **Relocation, doesn't work out** | 40% of relocations. Hire terminated at month 4, hiring restarts from zero. Where the math gets severe. |

### Breakeven Timeline

* Local hire (best case): Month 2
* Relocation hire (best case): Month 3

Relocation hires take one month longer on average because they're splitting focus between the job and figuring out where to live. Remove that friction with structured arrival support, and they hit breakeven on the same timeline as a local hire.

### Model Assumptions

* Benefits + payroll tax: 25% added to base salary
* Year-1 all-in cost: (Salary × 1.25) + $10K relocation bonus
* Ramp timeline: 9 months to full productivity (Deel)
* Relocation failure rate: 40% (Jobera)
* Value factor: expected annual output as a multiple of all-in cost (role-dependent, GetDX 2025 and QuotaPath)

## Productivity & Ramp

* **15–20 days of lost work focus** per standard relocation (Altair Global Relocation Productivity Study, 2015 — directional)
* **Average ramp time: 6–12 months**, model uses 9 months (Deel)
* **$11.5K wasted per $200K hire** = 3 weeks of salary burned on housing search instead of onboarding (\~$800/day × 15 working days)
* **DIY relocation total hidden cost: $10K–$20K per hire** = direct productivity loss ($12K) + manager time drain ($5K) + team coordination cost ($3K)
* Relocation friction cascades into revenue: sales pipelines delay, product velocity drops, customer response times increase

## Relocation Failure Rate

* **40% of relocations fail** (Jobera) — primary stat for the Ramp Calculator and content
* **Failed international assignment cost: $850K–$1.25M** (KPMG estimate, cited in International SOS/Ipsos ROI Study)
* Atlas Corporate Relocation Survey (2024, 575 decision-makers): 58% of companies experienced employee relocation declines in 2024 (vs. 64% in 2023); family responsibilities is the #1 reason employees decline
* Jobera Research: "Companies that invest in professional relocation support see significantly lower attrition in the first 12 months."

## Turnover & Replacement Costs

Gallup replacement cost by role band:

* Frontline / entry-level employees: \~40% of salary
* Mid-level employees: \~80% of salary
* Technical professionals: \~80% of salary
* Senior / specialized / leaders / managers: \~200% of salary

Work Institute 2023 Retention Report: turnover cost ≈ 33% of base pay (conservative)

Breakeven logic: for a $120K technical hire, enhanced relocation support ($10K more than DIY) breaks even if it reduces relocation-driven attrition risk by \~10 percentage points.

## Relocation Stress

* Moving ranks among the most stressful life events — same range as major work changes on the Social Readjustment Rating Scale (modern UK update: 32 Life Change Units, up from 20 in 1967)
* New Zealand longitudinal study (8 years): higher probability of moving associated with substantially higher prevalence of depression and anxiety
* WHO/ILO: 12 billion working days lost annually to depression and anxiety; $1 trillion/year in lost productivity globally
* Gallup: workers with fair/poor mental health have \~12 unplanned absence days/year vs. \~2.5 for those with better mental health
* Housing insecurity increases likelihood of forced move within one year by 39% (Australian Housing and Urban Research Institute)

## NYC Market Specifics

* NYC apartments rent in 48–72 hours — best options gone without someone local
* Landlord requirements: 40× rent in annual income, 700+ US credit score, SSN and US bank account
* No US credit = landlord denials without guidance; guarantor fee can equal one full month's rent
* Average 1-bed in NYC: \~$4K/month; first month + security = \~$8K upfront
* Typical DIY apartment search: 3–4 weeks, with real ramp-delay risk. NYCbound doesn't promise a specific turnaround time — what we guarantee is housing secured before Day One and the lost ramp time recovered.

## How to Use These Numbers in Messaging

**In cold outreach (Email 2–3):**<br>Reference the failed-relo cost for the role closest to your prospect's hires. For SWE prospects: "40% of relocations fail. When that happens, you're looking at $701K in missed Year 1 value compared to a hire that would have worked out." Offer the calculator as a value-add — not a pitch.

**In discovery:**<br>Use the calculator to align on economic stakes before pricing. Ask: "What did your last relocation that didn't work out actually cost you?"

**In content / pitch decks:**<br>The full scenario table is the core of the Hidden Cost narrative. Lead with the $701K gap between a working and failed relocation.

**The comparison that closes:**<br>Structured support (NYCbound) = \~$15K–$46K depending on tier and salary band. Missed Year 1 value on a failed relo = $701K. "The math is not close."

**Reach for the KPMG parallel** when you need a third-party anchor for the size of the number: "KPMG puts failed international assignment cost at $850K–$1.25M. We're in the same range."

**Never:**

* Lead with specific numbers or the calculator in cold Email 1. Earn it with the story first.
* Cite Jobera/KPMG/Atlas/Gallup inline in cold emails. Use "our research" instead. See [Email Plays](https://pinpointe.fibery.io/Sales_Intelligence/480) for the full attribution rule.
