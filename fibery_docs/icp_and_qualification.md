# ICP and Qualification

Use this when evaluating whether a company is a strong NYCbound prospect, prioritizing outreach, or building a target list.

---

## Primary & Secondary ICP

**Primary ICP — Mid-Market (Series C+):** Series C+, 200–2,000 employees (sweet spot: 500–1,500). Stable, well-funded, hiring repeatedly into NYC, NOT yet locked into RMC contracts. Policies are still being built. Sales cycles are weeks to months.

**Avoid:** 2,000+ employees — deprioritize regardless of how well an account scores on other dimensions. Sales cycles run 1–3 years and enterprise buyers are risk-averse careerists. Only pursue with a warm intro or a clear inbound signal. Sub-300 employees with one-off, informal relocation history is also a poor fit — conversion rates are too low to justify cold outreach.

---

## Hard Requirements

A company must meet ALL of these to be a prospect:

1. **Real NYC office.** Either:
   * Published NYC office address on corporate site, OR
   * Third-party lease reporting indicating multi-year commitment or sizable footprint (5K–20K+ SF, 5–10 year terms)

   Stronger qualifier: NYC roles repeatedly labeled hybrid/on-site AND spanning multiple functions, not just one sales role.
2. **200–2,000 employees.**
3. **Active NYC hiring.** At minimum one current NYC-based role. Strong qualifier: 5+ concurrent NYC openings.

---

## Hard Disqualifiers (Skip Entirely)

* No real NYC office (only a "mailbox" address with no hybrid/on-site jobs and no lease signals)
* Fully remote company where "location: New York" is only a tax/payroll anchor
* Sub-200 employees with NO relocation history
* Confirmed long-term RMC contract in place (enterprise)
* Repeated "relocation support is not available for this position" across NYC postings
* Repeated "work visa/immigration sponsorship is not available" across NYC postings
* Recruiter targets primarily roles under $150K base (SDR, BDR, coordinator, associate) — companies don't pay to relocate at this comp level

## Scoring Framework

Score each account 1–5 across five dimensions. Total out of 25.

| Dimension | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| **NYC hiring velocity** | Volume and recency of NYC-tagged open roles. Repeated backfills. Roles spanning multiple functions. |
| **Non-local talent dependency** | Senior roles recruited nationally. Relocation language in postings. Roles that can't be filled locally. |
| **International complexity** | Visa sponsorship offered. International hire patterns. Immigration coordination in People/HR jobs. |
| **Operational pain** | Evidence of People Ops stretched thin. No Global Mobility function yet. Ad hoc relocation handling. |
| **Willingness/ability to pay** | Stage (Series C+), revenue, explicit relocation packages or mobility programs mentioned publicly. |

### Tier Thresholds

* **Tier 1 (21–25):** Engage immediately. Top priority. Cold outreach acceptable for Mid-Market; warm intro for Enterprise.
* **Tier 2 (16–20):** Strong fit. Sequence in. Cold outreach acceptable for Mid-Market.
* **Tier 3 (11–15):** Monitor. Engage on stronger signal (e.g. new GM hire, NYC office expansion).
* **Disqualified (0–10):** Skip.

### Benchmark Examples

* **Celonis** (\~2,000 employees, 20+ offices, NYC = US HQ, explicit mobility program): 4/5 on dimension fit — illustrates strong NYC hiring velocity, international complexity, and formal mobility presence.
* **Criteo** (\~2,600 employees, $1.9B revenue, "very active global mobility program"): 3/5 on dimension fit — moderate fit on the scoring dimensions.
* **Unity** (\~4,400 employees, repeated "no relocation/no visa" across NYC roles): 2/5 on dimension fit — the stated no-relocation policy would be a hard disqualifier on its own regardless of headcount.

**Important caveat on all three:** Celonis, Criteo, and Unity are all 2,000+ employees — they illustrate how to score the five dimensions, but none of them actually qualify as ICP once the 2,000-employee ceiling is applied. Don't use these as target examples; use them only to understand the scoring mechanics.

## Positive Signals (Ranked by Tier)

### Tier 1 — Highest-Conviction Signals

* **First Global Mobility or Immigration hire posting.** Highest signal — they're formalizing relocation and haven't locked in vendors yet. This is a *stage signal*, not a timing trigger. Add to watchlist when the posting goes live. Reach out once the person is in seat, \~30-60 days after hire. Emailing before they start means there's no decision-maker on the other end yet.
* **New People Ops / HR leader hired.** Fresh mandate, open to change. Engage within 2-4 weeks of hire. 62% more receptive to outreach.
* **NYC office opening or significant expansion.** Real estate commitment = sustained hiring intent. Engage within 2 weeks of announcement.

### Tier 2 — Strong Signal, Sequence In

* 10+ concurrent open NYC roles (20+ = high conviction)
* Series C+ funding announcement with NYC hiring tied to it
* Visa sponsorship language in NYC job postings
* RTO mandate or "NYC required" added to postings
* Relocation assistance language in job postings

### Tier 3 — Supportive Evidence

* Senior NYC roles (Head of Industry, Field CTO, Director-level Finance, Principal Engineer)
* Decision-maker recently changed roles
* HR/People blog posts about internal mobility or international hires
* Structured hybrid policies (2 days/week in office)
* Recent NYC lease expansion or renewal

### Positive keywords to scan

"relocation package," "relocation assistance," "mobility program," "global mobility," "internal mobility," "international hires," "expatriates," "NYC office," "hybrid – New York," "on-site – New York," "Global Mobility & Immigration"

---

## Warning Signs (Deprioritize, Watch for Change)

* 0–3 open NYC roles with no hiring velocity
* No mention of global mobility or relocation in corporate filings, careers content, or HR blogs
* Low NYC hiring velocity overall
* Tenured GM (4+ years) with no newer mobility/immigration hires (status quo bias, established vendor relationships)

### Negative keywords

* "Relocation support is not available for this position."
* "Work visa/immigration sponsorship is not available for this position."

## Persona Sequencing & Multi-Threading

Once an account qualifies, decide who to contact and in what order. Multi-threading — reaching several stakeholders per account — is the default, not the exception. Committees buy, not individuals, and reaching only one person means your deal lives or dies on whether they forward your email internally (they usually won't). The discipline is being intentional: a differentiated message per persona, staggered timing, never the same email copy-pasted to five people.

### Quick Reference

| Qualifying signal | Lead persona (start here) | Then expand to | Max contacts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First GM hire | GM person (once in seat \~30-60 days) | People Ops, then CFO/Finance | 4-5 (enterprise) |
| New People Ops / HR leader | That People Ops leader (fresh-mandate window) | CEO/Founder or TA, then CFO | 2-3 MM / 4-5 ENT |
| NYC hiring velocity / volume (no person signal) | People Ops (MM) or CEO/Founder (200-500) | TA, then CFO/Finance | 2-3 (mid-market) |
| Enterprise, via warm intro | Whoever the intro lands on | Committee: GM, People Ops, Procurement, Total Rewards | 4-5 (enterprise) |

**Contact count by org size:**

* **Small / mid-market:** 2-3 — People Ops + Founder/CEO + maybe one more (TA, or Ops/Finance)
* **Larger / enterprise:** 4-5 max

### Lead personas vs expanders

Not every persona is a cold entry point.

* **Lead / entry personas:** People Ops/HR Leader, CEO/Founder, CFO/Finance (cold-entry as of the Sept 2026 play rebuild — the play now leads with a quantified number and a stated fix, not relationship-first messaging), Global Mobility (when signal-triggered), Recruiter/TA (when they're the loudest pain-owner or you can't identify People Ops).
* **Expanders / amplifiers (not cold entry):** Total Rewards — an influencer, never a starting point.

### What makes multi-threading thoughtful, not spammy

* **Differentiate the angle per persona.** CFO gets the quantified ramp-gap math (the rebuilt CFO play). People Ops gets candidate-experience-and-bandwidth. The GM person gets the NYC-exceptions framing. Recruiter/TA gets "protect your placements." CEO/Founder gets "protect the first 30 days." Never the same body text.
* **Waterfall or all at once, either works.** Stagger the lead persona first with others following over a few days, or reach every qualified contact together — timing isn't the thing that reads as spammy. What matters is differentiated copy per persona; sending the exact same body text to five people at once is what gets flagged as a blast, not the timing itself.
* **Reference the thread when natural.** "I also reached out to \[name\] on your team" reads as coordinated and signals you understand how their org buys — the opposite of spray-and-pray.
* **Respect the gatekeeper.** Multi-threading is NOT going over someone's head. It's reaching the gatekeeper AND the other committee stakeholders with role-appropriate messages, in parallel, keeping the gatekeeper central. (See the Squarespace approach: Reese stays the anchor; Procurement and Total Rewards get their own role-tuned outreach.)
* **Cap it.** 2-3 contacts for mid-market, 4-5 for enterprise. More than that at one company reads as a blast no matter how well-written.
